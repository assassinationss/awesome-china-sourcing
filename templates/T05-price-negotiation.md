# T05 · Price Negotiation Email

> The polite anchor. Uses MOQ tiers to make the seller negotiate against themselves.

**Subject: RE: Quotation — [Product] — let's align on structure**

```
Hello [Sales name],

Thanks for the quote. Candidly: $[X.XX]/unit at [MOQ] sits above the two other quotes on our shortlist ($[A.AA] and $[B.BB], same spec).

I'd rather build this with you — your certifications and lead time are the strongest of the three. So let's find the structure that works:

1. **Tiered pricing**: $[target] at [MOQ], $[target−Δ] at [2×MOQ], $[target−2Δ] at [4×MOQ]
2. **Price hold**: this pricing held for [6] months
3. If unit price is truly fixed — what can move instead? Tooling amortization, payment terms, or freight support at the next tier?

We're deciding by [date]. If the structure lands, the first PO follows this month.

Best regards,
[Name]
```

**Why it works:** competitive quotes stated flatly (not as a threat), tier structure invites them to compete with *themselves*, and the "what can move instead" line converts a price stalemate into a terms negotiation. The date creates pressure without ultimatum drama.
