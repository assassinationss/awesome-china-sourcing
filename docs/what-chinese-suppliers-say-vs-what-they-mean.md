# What Chinese Suppliers Say vs. What They Actually Mean — The Bilingual Decoder

> Every exporting culture has its codes. China's export desks speak a dialect of courtesy where "difficult" means "no", "maybe" means "no", and "no problem" means "I heard you". This decoder translates the twelve highest-stakes phrases — written from the Chinese side of the table, where they're understood literally.

You can speak fluent English and still be mis-translating every negotiation. The phrases below are not lies; they're a communication culture that avoids the hard "no" (保面子 — saving face), avoids conflict in writing, and treats ambiguity as politeness. Read them wrong and you'll think you have an agreement when you have a vague feeling. Read them right and you hold the actual answer in the first reply.

This is the negotiation layer of the [VERIFY system](../README.md#-the-verify-system) — after the supplier is real, these are the words that set the price and the date.

## The twelve phrases that matter

### 1. 有点难度 — "A bit difficult"

**What it sounds like:** a solvable problem.
**What it means:** No — or yes, at a price they don't want to name first.

谁先出价谁输 ("whoever names a number first loses") is the local instinct. "A bit difficult" is an invitation for *you* to make it easier — with money, volume, or timeline.

**Your move:** "I understand. What would make it easy?" — make them name the number. If they still won't, they can't, and the answer is no.

### 2. 这已经是最低价了 — "This is already our best price"

**What it means:** This is the best price *for this configuration, this quantity, this payment terms, right now.* Each variable is a lever.

**Your move:** Change a variable, not the sentence. +20% quantity, 30% deposit instead of bargaining the unit price — often a 3–7% drop appears "because the boss approved." The 5–10% that exists lives in quantity, payment terms, and mix — not in asking again.

### 3. 交期大概 25 天 — "Lead time is about 25 days"

**What it means:** Production is 25 days *after materials arrive and after the deposit clears and after Chinese New Year (if within 6 weeks)*. Freight is separate.

**Your move:** Ask the three-part question: "From deposit received to ex-factory — is 25 days all-in, including material procurement?" Then add your own buffer for the silence between "finished" and "actually shipped." Rule of thumb from the China side: mentally add 30–50% for first orders. And if CNY falls within 6 weeks of your goods-ready date, run the [CNY 2027 Deadline Calculator](https://assassinationss.github.io/cny-deadline.html) — the shutdown canyon is 3–5 weeks in practice, not the 8 official days.

### 4. 我们是大工厂 — "We are a big factory"

**What it means:** Often the opposite — or technically true (the group is big) while your order runs on a small line. Big factories with real volume don't need to say it; their capacity and certifications show it.

**Your move:** Don't argue — verify. Ask for the business license ([how to read it](how-to-read-a-chinese-business-license.md)) and check registered capital and business scope, then ask which production lines will run your goods and the factory pickup address under FOB. Real factories answer in minutes.

### 5. 没问题 — "No problem"

**What it means:** "I heard you." It is an acknowledgment, not a commitment. The commitment is the PI (proforma invoice) with the spec written down.

**Your move:** Convert instantly: "Great — please send the PI with that spec and date." The speed of the PI is the truth test. "No problem" followed by PI delay means there was a problem.

### 6. MOQ 是 1000 — "MOQ is 1,000"

**What it means:** MOQ is 1,000 for this price. First orders of 500–600 are frequently possible at +5–10%, or with a "trial order" framing that costs a relationship point instead of money.

**Your move:** "This is a first order; the reorder is where we scale. Can we do 500 at a fair trial price?" — suppliers who refuse all flexibility on MOQ at order #1 are telling you about orders #2–#10.

### 7. 老板不在 / 要问一下老板 — "The boss isn't in / I need to ask the boss"

**What it means:** One of three things: genuinely out (common — owners of SME factories travel), a stalling tactic while they shop your ask internally, or "the boss" is the fictional final authority every negotiator keeps in reserve.

**Your move:** Never pressure the boss's schedule — that's a face attack. Set a time instead: "Of course — when can you get back to me on this?" A date for the answer is the real commitment.

### 8. 这是行业惯例 — "This is industry standard practice"

**What it means:** It benefits them. Industry standards that favor the buyer are never invoked — they're just silently applied.

**Your move:** "Happy to follow standard practice — send me a reference or the standard document." Standards that exist survive documentation. Most evaporate at this request.

### 9. 我们都是这么操作的 — "This is how we always do it"

**Cousin of #8**, with a twist: it often precedes a request that deviates from what *you* should accept — paying to a personal account, 100% T/T upfront, skipping the inspection "since we're experienced."

**Your move:** "I'm sure — and we'll keep the parts that protect both of us: company account, 30/70, third-party QC." See [how to pay safely](how-to-pay-a-chinese-supplier-safely-2026.md). The methods that are "how we always do it" and legitimate survive standardization; the ones that don't were never standard.

### 10. 马上给你答复 — "I'll get back to you right away"

**What it means:** Within 2–3 business days, if nothing upstream stalls. "Right away" in export-sales time is a mood, not a unit.

**Your move:** Anchor it: "Great — if I don't hear by Friday I'll assume the price doesn't work and move on." Deadlines get replies; politeness gets silence. This one sentence is worth a chapter.

### 11. 这批货有一点点色差，不影响使用 — "Slight color variation, doesn't affect use"

**What it means:** There is a defect batch and they hope you'll absorb it. "Doesn't affect use" is their assessment, not yours — and it transfers the cost of their QC miss to your listing.

**Your move:** The quality-claim template exists for exactly this: photos, batch numbers, and the disposition options (rework / discount / replacement) — with the cost of each on their side. Verbal reassurance is not a disposition.

### 12. 不好意思，最近原材料涨价了 — "Sorry, raw material prices went up"

**What it means:** Sometimes true (commodity inputs genuinely swing), sometimes the annual ritual price-grab before peak season. Your leverage: real material indices are public.

**Your move:** "Understood — which material? Let's index that input in the price so we both share the movement both ways." Suppliers who only pass increases, never decreases, will refuse an index — and now you know what the "increase" was.

## The meta-rule

Every phrase above resolves the same way: **move the conversation from words to documents.** PIs with specs, indexed prices, dated answers, written dispositions. The supplier's courtesy dialect is not deception — it's how the market talks. The importer's advantage is not speaking louder English; it's converting every "no problem" into paper.
